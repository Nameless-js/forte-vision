### Candidate type

**Primary: `accept_without_count` — «Приём наличных без полистного пересчёта» (в присутствии клиента).**
Reasoning: the client hands over cash (7–8.5 s) and the cashier performs no banknote-by-banknote count and no machine count while the client is at the desk (8.5–11.5 s, only a ~2 s hold/look at 9–10.5 s). The client turns away at 11.5 s and is at the door by 12.5 s; only then does the cashier feed the notes into the counting machine (12.5–13 s). The labeled second (13 s) is exactly the frame where the cashier's hand is on the machine hopper while the client is leaving — i.e. the moment that proves the acceptance was completed without a count in the client's presence. The staged script is clearly "client drops off money and leaves; cashier counts afterwards".

**Precise t_event = 12.5 s.** At 11.5 s the client has turned his back with the cash still uncounted in the cashier's hands (earliest anchor); at 12.5 s the cashier starts moving the notes to the machine with the client already at the door — the first frame in which "counting without the client" is visible; at 13.0 s (label) the notes are in the hopper. A detector keyed on "client left the desk zone while the cash is uncounted" would fire at 11.5–12.0 s; one keyed on "count starts with no client present" fires at 12.5–13.0 s.

Alternatives:

- `no_id_check` — «Операция без предъявления документа». Also objectively present: the client takes only the cash from his pocket (6 s) and never shows a card/document before the cashier takes the money (8.5 s). If the organisers' label marked this, it would be expected near 7–9 s, not 13 s; so it is secondary, but a rule set should treat this clip as positive for the missing ID step as well.
- `other` — "client leaves before the operation is complete / cash handled outside the client's presence" is really the same fact as the primary category seen from the client's side; mentioned only because the dataset may name it separately.
- `issue_without_count` — rejected: the cash moves client → cashier (client's hand places it at 7 s, cashier picks it up at 8.5 s, feeds the machine at 13 s); the cashier's open-hand gesture at 11 s hands nothing over.
- `unauthorized_in_cash_zone` — rejected: the client leans over the far-left end of the desk (7–8 s) and touches its far-left corner (12 s); he never crosses to the near (cashier) side, the safe or any drawer.

Confidence in the primary category: ~0.6 (the counting-after-departure reading fits the label time best; the missing ID step is the main competing interpretation).

### Detection cues

1. **Client track vs. count event.** Track the client's bounding box; define "at the desk" as the far-side desk ROI. Here the client is at the desk 7.0–11.5 s, in the door ROI 12.5–14.5 s, gone after 14.5 s. Define a *count event* as either (a) hand-count: both hands over a stack with repeated small thumb/finger motions for ≥ 2–3 s, or (b) machine-count: cashier's hand in the hopper ROI on top of the counting machine (right end of the desk in this video). Violation rule: cash object accepted by the cashier AND no count event while the client is in the desk ROI AND (client leaves the desk ROI OR the count starts after he leaves). Fires at 11.5–12.5 s in this clip.
2. **Cash object.** Small light rectangular blob: appears from the client's hand onto the desk (7 s), transfers to the cashier's hand (8–9 s), persists in her hand at chest height (9–12 s), travels to the hopper ROI (12.5–13 s). Object-in-hand duration without counting motion (~4 s) is itself a flag.
3. **Pose.** Cashier's right arm raised and extended to the right toward the machine (12.5–16 s) while the client's box is in the door ROI / outside the room — a cheap keypoint rule (right wrist above shoulder and right of the torso) combined with "no client in desk ROI".
4. **Missing-ID step.** No second small object (card) appears on the desk or is held up between the client's arrival and the cash pickup; the only hand-to-desk motion of the client (7 s) carries the cash.
5. **Timing.** Handover-to-departure interval ≈ 4.5 s (7.0 → 11.5 s) — too short for any counting; a very short desk dwell after cash handover is a useful prior.

### Look-alike normal actions (FP risks)

- **Normal machine count with the client present** gives the identical cashier pose (arm to the hopper); the only difference is the client's position. The rule must be conditioned on the client track, not on the pose alone.
- **Client leaving after a completed operation**: the cashier may put already-counted notes into the machine/safe after the client exits. Distinguish by a count event earlier in the client-present interval.
- **Brief hold/look at the notes (9–10.5 s)** can be mistaken for counting by motion-energy detectors; require leaf-by-leaf finger motion or machine use.
- **Light rectangular object in the hand (9–10 s)** can be misclassified as a smartphone (`phone_at_workplace` FP). It is the cash stack; the only phone in the scene is the desk telephone at the far-left corner.
- **Client leaning over the desk (7–8 s) and touching the desk corner (12 s)**: both on the far/left end — not a cash-zone intrusion.
- **Cashier's open-hand gesture toward the client (11 s)** resembles handing cash to the client (issue FP); no object leaves her hand.
- **People behind the glass partition (1–2, 9–12, 15–17 s)** are in the adjacent room — exclude the glass area from "second client" logic.
