### Normal protocol observed
This clip contains no client operation; it defines the NORMAL "finish and leave the workplace" routine:
1. 0–3 s — cashier works seated at her desk (reviews a booklet / document); no cash is on the desk at any time.
2. 3–4.5 s — closes the document; nothing is left open or spread on the desk.
3. 4.5–7.5 s — stands up and opens the safe herself; she stays within arm's reach of the desk and the safe the whole time.
4. 8–10 s — takes the document from the desk and puts it INSIDE the safe before doing anything else.
5. 11–12 s — closes the safe door between the two storage actions (the door is not left standing open while she goes back to the desk).
6. 13–17 s — takes the second item (teal container) from the desk and puts it inside the safe.
7. 18–19 s — closes the safe door.
8. 20–24 s — locks the safe: a long (≈5 s) two-handed interaction at the lock / handle, cashier facing the safe.
9. 25–27 s — only after the safe is locked and the desk is clear does she walk to the glass door and leave.
10. 28–31 s — end state: no cash or documents on the desk, safe closed, nobody in the room. This is the correct end state.

- Hand-object cues: both hands on an A4-size booklet 0–3 s; right hand carrying the booklet 8–10 s; right hand carrying a small cylinder 13–16 s; empty hands from 18 s; no phone-shaped object at any time.

### Look-alike normal actions (FP risks)
- Standing up and leaving the desk when no client is present (this clip 4.5–7.5 s and 25–27 s) vs. cashier_left_during_service (requires a client being served at the desk).
- Opening the safe and bending into it (10 s, 17 s) vs. cash_left_unattended / open safe while away (requires the cashier to be absent, or at distance with the door open or cash on the desk).
- Leaning over the desk from the cashier's side (8 s, 13 s) vs. unauthorized_in_cash_zone (requires a non-cashier body or hand crossing the line into the cashier's side).
- Holding a small object near the chest and looking at it (13–15 s, teal container) vs. phone_at_workplace (a phone is a thin dark slab held screen-to-face, often with thumb motion).
- Turning pages of a booklet (0–3 s) vs. banknote counting by hand — both are fast repeated leaf motions; the booklet is larger (A4-ish), has a dark cover and printed pictures.
- A closed dark booklet lying on the desk (3–8 s) vs. a tablet / phone lying on the desk.
- Reflection in the glass partition (≈12–16 s, ≈24–26 s) vs. a second person entering.
- In other clips the same "cashier stands, opens the safe, stores items" sequence performed WHILE a client sits at the desk with cash out would be a different situation; the discriminating feature is client presence and cash on the desk, not the safe interaction itself.
