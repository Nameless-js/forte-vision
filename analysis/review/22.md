### Candidate type

**Chosen id: `cashier_left_during_service` — «Кассир покинул рабочее место до окончания обслуживания клиента», read in its broad form «Кассир покинул рабочее место / кассовый узел оставлен без присмотра».**

Reasoning:
- The only abnormal, visually clear event in the clip is the cashier abandoning the workplace: she walks out of the cash room and the desk (open laptop, counting machine, papers) and the safe stay with nobody present from 17.5 s to the end of the clip (≥ 3.5 s, clip ends while she is still away).
- All client-related categories are impossible here: there is no client, no cash, no document, no bag, no second person (`issue_without_count`, `accept_without_count`, `no_id_check`, `collection_bag_unchecked`, `two_clients_cash_mixed`, `handover_bypassing_transfer_device` excluded; there is also no tray in the scene).
- `unauthorized_in_cash_zone` excluded: nobody enters; the silhouettes in the glass at 12–17 s are reflections that move with the cashier, and the pink figure behind the glass at 18–19 s is the cashier herself outside.
- `phone_at_workplace` excluded: at 13.5–17 s the hands stay at waist/hip level, the head is up and turned toward the wall/door rather than bowed, no phone-shaped object is seen, and at 17 s both hands are on the door handle.
- The label time fits the start of the departure: hand off the safe 13.5 s, stationary at 14.0 s, rotation toward the exit 14.5 s, walking 15 s, door crossed 17–17.5 s. A labeler marking "cashier leaves the workplace" at the moment she detaches from the safe/desk lands on 14 s.
- Caveat: the literal clause "while the client is still being served" is **not** met (no client). The organisers' real category is therefore probably a workplace-abandonment type without the client condition; the id above is the closest item in the given list.

Alternatives, in order of plausibility:
1. **`cash_left_unattended` — «Наличные оставлены без присмотра»** (p ≈ 0.30). Would be the organisers' intent if the staged script is "cashier handles the safe lock (locks or *un*locks it) and leaves the cash room". Visually the safe door is closed in every frame and the keyhole looks unchanged before/after, no cash lies on the desk, so the visual evidence does not support the literal definition ("cash out or safe open"); the locked/unlocked state cannot be determined from video. If the organisers' list has a "safe/cash storage left unattended" item, this clip could be it, and the detection cues below are the same.
2. **`other` — workstation left unlocked / cash zone left with equipment active** (p ≈ 0.10). The laptop stays open with a lit screen while the room is empty (18–21 s); at 6–7 s she touches the lid but does not close it. Not in the candidate list; mention for completeness.
3. `phone_at_workplace` (p < 0.05) — only because the hands are hidden in front of the body at 14–15.5 s; rejected on posture (see above).

**Precise t_event = 14.5 s.** Justification: 13.5 s is the last contact with the safe; 14.0 s she is already detached from both desk and safe but stationary (this is the frame the organisers chose); 14.5 s is the first frame in which the body rotates toward the exit, i.e. the departure becomes unambiguous; 15.0 s she is walking; 17.0–17.5 s she crosses the door line; 18.0 s the room is empty. For a rule that fires on "cashier track leaves the workplace zone" the natural onset is 14.5 s (±0.5 s), with confirmation at 17.5 s (room empty).

### Detection cues

- **Person tracking / zone logic.** One tracked person in the cashier zone (polygon: desk + cashier chair + safe pedestal). Event = that track leaves the zone through the door region and no person remains in the zone; raise when the empty state lasts > ~2 s (here it lasts from 17.5 s to the end). Onset marker = the track's velocity vector pointing to the door while the person is standing (14.5–15 s).
- **Pose cues.** Standing up from the chair (hip/shoulder key-points rise ~7 s), upright walking gait with torso facing the door, arm reaching to the door handle (16 s), body crossing the door line (17 s). Head up, hands below chest — distinguishes from phone use.
- **Safe-interaction precursor.** Hand key-point within the safe-door keyhole ROI for ≥ 3 s (9–13.5 s), followed within < 2 s by departure. This "touch safe → leave" sequence is the strongest discriminator for this scenario and also covers the alternative reading (safe left unlocked). The door-open state can be checked by an edge/intensity change along the door's left edge — here it never changes.
- **Equipment state.** Laptop screen ROI stays bright while no person is in the zone (18–21 s) — supports a "workplace left active/unattended" rule.
- **Absence of transactional cues.** No second person, no hand-to-hand motion over the desk, no objects on the desk surface change; a rule for this type should not require cash detection.
- **Reflection suppression.** The glass partition produces person-like reflections (12–17 s) and the exterior figure (18–19 s). Mask the glass area for presence counting, or require feet on the carpet inside the room.

### Look-alike normal actions (FP risks)

- Cashier walks to the safe to deposit/withdraw cash and **returns** to the desk (track leaves the desk but stays in the zone, no door crossing).
- Cashier stands up to reach the counting machine, the printer-like device or papers at the left end of the desk.
- Cashier goes to the door to let a client in or to close it and comes back within ~3 s (short absence; add a minimum empty-zone duration and prefer re-entry logic).
- End-of-shift locking of the safe followed by leaving — visually identical sequence; only context (shift schedule, no open operation) separates it from a violation. Any rule will flag it; whether that is desired is a policy question.
- Cashier leaning far right behind the laptop/safe so that the track is occluded by the safe or the foreground desk — can look like "zone empty". Use a track-lost grace period and the door region as the only exit path.
- Static person seen in the adjacent room through the glass (0–11 s here) and reflections in the glass — can be miscounted as a client or an unauthorized person.

### Uncertainties

- Whether the key action at 9–13.5 s locked or unlocked the safe cannot be determined; the door is closed in every sampled frame (0.5 s steps between 12 and 16 s, 1 s elsewhere). A very brief open/close between samples is physically unlikely for a safe door but not strictly excluded.
- The small object in the right hand is presumed to be a key; it is a few pixels in size and cannot be identified.
- What the left hand does in front of the body at 14.0–14.5 s is hidden (most plausibly pocketing the key); a small phone cannot be fully excluded, but posture argues against phone use.
- No client is present, so the mapping to the given candidate list is approximate; the organisers' own category name is unknown. `cashier_left_during_service` (broad reading) and `cash_left_unattended` (safe-related reading) describe the same visible event.
- The clip ends at 21 s with the cashier still absent; the total length of the absence is unknown.
- The laptop screen content and lock state cannot be read.
- The cashier sits on the far side of the desk in this video (differs from the typical staging); if detectors use a fixed "cashier seat" ROI, this video will not match it.
