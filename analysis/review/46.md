### Detection cues

- **Object cue:** appearance of a cup/bottle/food-wrapper class object on the cash desk or in the cashier's hands (lidded takeaway cup, bottle with coloured cap, glossy snack wrapper) while the person is at the cash position. Here: cup first visible at 11.0 s, bottle ~9.0 s, wrapper ~7.0 s.
- **Pose/motion cue:** repeated hand-to-mouth motion with an object held in the hand (drinking/eating), sustained or repeated over several seconds — here 4 sips within 14–17.5 s. A single hand-to-face touch is not enough; require the object in hand plus ≥2 repetitions or ≥1.5 s at the mouth.
- **Context cue:** no client present / no cash-operation objects (banknotes, documents) active — personal-consumption objects plus an idle service context strengthen the call.
- **Retrieval cue:** reaching behind/over the counting machine to a zone where no service object lives, ending with a new object in hand (0–1 s, 8–11 s here).

### Look-alike normal actions (FP risks)

- Loading or emptying the **counting machine hopper** involves the same reach over the machine top (as at 0–1 s and 8 s here); distinguish by what comes back in the hand — banknotes (thin, flexible, fanned) vs a cylindrical cup/bottle.
- A **banknote bundle** on the desk can resemble the flat yellow snack pack at this resolution; colour plus the glossy wrapper texture and the absence of any client/transaction are the separators.
- Hand raised to the **face/hair** (she adjusts hair in other videos) mimics a sip; require an object in the hand at mouth height.
- Holding a **stamp, receipt roll or ink pad** near the chest could look like holding a cup in low resolution; a lidded cup is taller and is tilted towards the mouth when drinking.
- The **white/blue small object at 9–10 s** could be mistaken for a phone by a naive detector; it is a bottle/cup (later stands upright on the desk).
