### Event at the label

[7 s – 13 s]:

- 7–8 s: the client stands at the far-left end of the desk; his right hand goes to the jacket pocket and comes out holding a small light-blue item (8 s).
- 8.5–9.0 s: while lowering himself onto the chair he stretches his right arm across the desk and lays the item on the desk centre (slightly on his own side of the midline). He does not hold anything up for the cashier to look at and nothing else is put on the desk.
- 9.5–10.0 s: the item lies alone on the desk; the client's hands rest at the far edge; the cashier lifts her right hand from the laptop (10.0 s = label).
- 10.5 s: the cashier's right hand reaches the item and takes it — this is the acceptance of the cash.
- 11.0 s: she lifts it in her right hand; 11.5–13 s she holds it with both hands in front of her chest and looks at it; no leafing motion, no machine yet.

So within t_label ± 3 s the full handover happens: cash goes client → desk → cashier, with no document presented or examined at any point before or during it. Immediately afterwards (14–15 s) the cashier loads the cash into the counting machine, so counting is not what is missing.

### Candidate type

- **id:** `no_id_check` — "Операция без предъявления документа".
- **Reasoning:** The client's first and only action after reaching the desk is to put cash on it (9 s), and the cashier accepts it (10.5 s) and goes on to count it in the machine (14–15 s). At no moment between the client's entry (4 s) and the acceptance does anyone hold up, pass, open or look at an identity document; the cashier does not type anything into the laptop after the client sits, does not receive and return a card. The organisers' timestamp (10 s) sits exactly on the handover window (9.0–10.5 s), which is the moment at which an "operation without a document" becomes observable. The subsequent machine loading rules out the "no counting" family, and the cashier never leaves, never opens the safe, never uses a phone.
- **Alternatives:**
  - `accept_without_count` — "Приём наличных без полистного пересчёта": less likely. The cashier does not store the cash; she places it into the counting-machine hopper at 14 s, which is a count by machine. It would only apply if the organisers required a manual sheet-by-sheet count in front of the client in addition to the machine, or if the machine run never happens after the clip ends (not visible). The label at 10 s also precedes any storage.
  - `other` — "cash accepted without any accompanying paperwork (no slip, no signature)": describes the same observable gap as `no_id_check` and the same moment; it is a wording alternative rather than a different event.
  - `unauthorized_in_cash_zone` — rejected: the client's arm reaches only to the desk centre at 9 s (a normal handover reach), never to the cashier's side, the safe or the machine.
  - `phone_at_workplace` — rejected: the two-handed hold at 11.5–13 s looks phone-like, but the item was received from the client and ends up in the counting machine.
- **Precise t_event:** 10.5 s. The violation is the acceptance of cash with no prior document check, and the acceptance becomes visible when the cashier's hand closes on the cash at 10.5 s (reach starts at 10.0 s; the cash was laid down by the client at 9.0 s). Any value in 9.0–10.5 s is defensible; 10.5 s is the instant the cash is in the cashier's hand.

### Detection cues

- Person tracks: a second person enters through the top-left door, walks to the far side and sits on the far chair (arrival 4–9 s). Cashier stays seated on the near side the whole time.
- Small object on the desk centre: a bright/light-coloured rectangle appears on the empty desk surface between the two people (9.0–10.5 s), placed by the client's hand and lifted by the cashier's hand — a client→cashier handover event.
- Cashier right-arm reach across the desk toward the far side (10.0–10.5 s), then both hands together at chest height (11.5–13 s), then right arm fully extended to the desk's left end with the hand over the counting-machine top (14–15 s). The sequence "handover → both-hand hold → hand over counting machine" within ~5 s marks the object as cash.
- Negative cue (the actual violation): no "document presentation" pattern anywhere between the client sitting (8.5 s) and the cash pickup (10.5 s): no object held up at face/eye level toward the cashier, no object passed cashier→client back, no cashier head-down-at-card followed by typing, no second small object on the desk. A rule: first client→cashier object that reaches the counting machine (or safe) with zero preceding document events → `no_id_check`.
- Durations: client seated 1.5 s before the cash is taken; total handover 1.5 s; inspection 2 s; machine loading 2 s. The whole operation is very short, which is itself a cue (a proper ID step adds several seconds of card handling before any cash moves).

### Look-alike normal actions (FP risks)

- A legitimate ID presentation looks almost identical to 9–13 s here: a client lays a small light-blue card on the desk, the cashier picks it up and looks at it in both hands. The two are separated only by what happens next (card returned to the client or laid beside the laptop, vs. cash sent to the counting machine or safe). A detector must therefore not flag the handover itself; it must check for the absence of a document step before a cash step.
- The cashier's two-handed hold at chest height (11.5–13 s) resembles smartphone use — `phone_at_workplace` false positive risk.
- The cashier's long reach to the counting machine (14–15 s) is normal counting behaviour, not a violation; it must not be read as "reaching into the client zone" or "cash left somewhere".
- The person in pink visible through the glass door (0–3 s, 9–17 s) is outside the room; a person counter that includes the background room would see "two clients" (`two_clients_cash_mixed` false positive).
- The client's arm stretched across the desk at 9 s (to the centre line) can trip an over-wide "cashier zone" and produce an `unauthorized_in_cash_zone` false positive.
- The client standing at the far end of the desk for ~2 s before sitting (6–8 s) with his hand in his pocket is normal arrival behaviour.

### Uncertainties

- Object identity is inferred from behaviour (it is loaded into the counting-machine hopper and never comes back), not from appearance: at 464x848 the light-blue rectangle could equally be a plastic ID card. If it is in fact an ID card, the scene would be a document handover rather than a cash acceptance, and the labeled violation would have to be something else (e.g. the cashier placing the client's document on the counting machine), which is not a listed category.
- The counting machine is loaded at 14–15 s, but the clip ends at 17.1 s before any machine run, count result, storage or return is visible; the `accept_without_count` alternative cannot be fully excluded on this basis.
- Whether the client took the item from his jacket pocket at 7 s is plausible but not clearly resolved.
- Nothing on the laptop screen or on the item is readable; no audio.
- Other suspicious moments outside the label: none observed.
